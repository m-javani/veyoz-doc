What's in the query

Filtering

10,000 properties reduced to ~300 candidates by district, with room-type and bed filters applied. Microseconds, not milliseconds.

Pricing

Each surviving property is priced for the query: rate, child supplements, charges, and promotions. The pricing step reads from a per-query in-memory view, not the underlying data.

Allocation

For multi-room queries, the engine picks the cheapest combination that covers the party. The 2-room case shows the extra cost: an additional ~370 µs at p50.

Response assembly

The winning rooms are priced once more to collect per-room promotion and excluded-tax detail. This is bounded by the number of rooms returned.

Caveats

These are honest numbers from modest hardware.

  • · Laptop-class CPU. Production servers have more cores and wider memory bandwidth.
  • · Engine only. HTTP, JSON, and network latency are excluded.
  • · Fixture is synthetic. Real property mixes vary. Filter tightness changes the number of candidates and therefore the time.

The engine prices thousands of candidate rate cells in under a millisecond, on a laptop — including filters, pricing, and allocation. The cost is dominated by how many candidates survive the filter, not how many properties exist.
